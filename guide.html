Teacher’s Guide · Prototype, November 2026

Grandfather’s Puzzle

A study app built from Grandfather Is Dead. Students read three of the book’s thirty “puzzle pieces” and use them to prepare for the English section of the GSAT (學測).

Open the app

Getting started

  1. Open the link on any phone, tablet, or computer. Students do not need an account or a download.
  2. The home page shows all thirty pieces of the book. Three are open for now: 1 Age, 2 Gratitude, and 11 Fear.
  3. Tap a piece. Each piece has five tabs across the top.
  4. On a phone, students can use “Add to Home Screen” so the app opens like a regular app.

The five tabs

  • Read閱讀

    Your original text, paragraph by paragraph, with Luke’s Chinese translation underneath. Students can hide the Chinese. Key words are underlined by GSAT level. Tapping a word shows an English definition, the Chinese meaning, and its level.

  • Words單字

    Flashcards for the words on the official GSAT list. The back of each card shows an English definition, the Chinese meaning, and the sentence from the book where the word appears. Students choose what the back shows: English and Chinese, English only, or Chinese only. Words beyond the GSAT list appear in a separate “understand, don’t memorize” list, also with English definitions.

  • Practice題型

    Twenty-two questions per piece in the GSAT formats: vocabulary, cloze, word bank, paragraph structure, reading comprehension, and a mixed-format section. Students get instant feedback with an explanation and a total score.

  • Writing寫作

    Two Chinese-to-English translation items with model answers, and a two-paragraph essay prompt in the GSAT style. Students can tap sentences from the book to insert them into their essay. A word counter and a self-check list are included.

  • Speaking口說

    Based on your Think, Pair, Share routine. Three discussion questions per piece, each with sentence starters, and one- and two-minute timers.

Using it in class

  1. Before class: students read the piece in the Read tab and review the flashcards.
  2. In class: project the Speaking tab and run Think, Pair, Share with the timer.
  3. After class: students complete the Practice tab and draft the essay in the Writing tab.

About student data. Answers, flashcard progress, and essay drafts are saved only in each student’s own browser. Nothing is sent to us, so teachers cannot see students’ scores. If a student clears the browser or switches devices, their progress starts over. If you would like to collect scores, let us know and we can add that.

What we would love to hear

This is a prototype, so honest reactions help most:

  • Are the questions at the right level for your students?
  • Are the English definitions clear enough?
  • Which tab did students use most, and which did they skip?
  • Which pieces should we add next?
